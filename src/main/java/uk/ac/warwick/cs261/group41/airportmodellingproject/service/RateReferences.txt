This text file provides references for the baseline rates for random events, which are based on real data.

==========================================================================================
Aircraft Rates:
==========================================================================================
Mechanical Failure Rate:
emergencyMechanicalBaselinePerTick = 0.000000135

How this is derived:
For passenger jet turbine engines, every 1 in 375000 hours there is an engine failure.
Reference: Rosen Aviation. How are airplanes able to fly with a failed engine? [Internet].
           Aviation; [cited 2026 Mar 14]. Available from: https://www.rosenaviation.com/blog/how-are-airplanes-able-to-fly-with-a-failed-engine/

33% of mechanical failures are due to engine failure.
Reference: Reveley MS, Briggs JL, Evans JK, Jones SM, Kurtoglu T, Leone KM, et al. Causal
           factors and adverse events of aviation accidents and incidents related to integrated
           vehicle health management. NASA/TM—2011-216967. Cleveland (OH): NASA Glenn Research Center; 2011 Mar.

Calculation: ((1/375000)/60)/0.33 = 0.000000135 chance of mechanical failures/minute

Passenger Health Emergency Rate:
emergencyHealthBaselinePerTick = 0.000000556

How this is derived:
Between January 2022 and December 2023, it was found that 1 in 212 flights involved a medical emergency.
Of those flights, 1.7% of the medical events were so serious that they caused the plane to divert.
Reference: Medical Xpress. Global analysis reveals the frequency of in-flight medical emergencies
           [Internet]. Medical Xpress; 2025 Sep 29 [cited 2026 Mar 14]. Available from: https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-09-global-analysis-reveals-frequency-flight.html

We assume an average flight time of 144 minutes (2h 24m).

Calculation: ((1/212)*0.017)/144 = 0.000000556 change of health emergencies requiring diversion/minute

Runway Rates:
==========================================================================================
Runway Inspection Rate:
failureInspectionBaselinePerTick = 0.001388

How this is derived:
Runways should be inspected at least daily, but one should be in darkness, so it is a good estimate to say 2 per day.
Reference: Federal Aviation Administration. Advisory Circular 150/5200-18C: Airport safety self-inspection
                  [Internet]. Washington (DC): US Department of Transportation, Federal Aviation Administration; 2004
                  Apr 23 [cited 2026 Mar 14]. Available from: https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/150_5200_18C.pdf

Calculation: (2/24)/60 = 0.001388 chance of inspection/minute

Snow Clearance Rate:
failureSnowBaselinePerTick = 0.00000951

How this is derived:
If we use Heathrow as an example of an airport in the UK, snow lies approximately 5 days per year.
Reference: Met Office. Southern England: climate [Internet]. Exeter: Met Office; 2016 [cited 2026 Mar 14]. Available from:
           https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/binaries/content/assets/metofficegovuk/pdf/weather/learn-about/weather/regional-climates/southern-england_-climate-met-office.pdf

Calculation: ((5/365)/24)/60 = 0.00000951 chance of snow/minute

Runway Failure Rate:
failureEquipmentBaselinePerTick = 0.00000278

How this is derived:
The MTTF for an incandescent runway light is 6000 hours.
Reference: Burns J, Dennie C, Elshetwy S, Lean D, Vigilante J. LED Airfield Lighting System Operation
           and Maintenance. Washington, DC: Transportation Research Board; 2015. ACRP Report 148.

The runway must close if two consecutive lights fail.
Reference: U.S. Department of Transportation, Federal Aviation Administration. Maintenance of
           airport visual aid facilities. Washington, DC: Federal Aviation Administration; 2014 Jun 20.
           144 p. Advisory Circular No.: 150/5340-26C.

Because the cause of failures are roughly correlated, we can assume that the rate of a runway closure would
be roughly equal to the rate of a single light failing.

Calculation: (1/6000)/60 = 0.00000278
